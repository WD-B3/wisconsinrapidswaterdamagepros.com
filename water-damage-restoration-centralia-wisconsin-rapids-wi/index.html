Centralia was its own city on the west side of the Wisconsin River until it consolidated with Grand Rapids in the spring of 1900, according to the Wisconsin Historical Society. The name is still on the map in the Centralia Dam on the river and in the Centralia Center, the city's senior resource center. As on the east side, we found no official boundary, so this page works from landmarks.

On the west bank the city lists Ben Hansen Park, the West River Bank Conservancy Area on 1st Avenue South, Mead Athletic Field and the Mead Splash Pad near Alton Street and 14th Avenue. Mead Elementary is at 241 17th Avenue South, and the Rapids Municipal Zoo is in the southwest corner of the city. They mark out the west bank that this page covers.

When water is spreading in Centralia

If water is deepening, touching the panel or coming up a floor drain, call at once. The dispatch line is open 24 hours a day, so a basement that begins to seep in the small hours gets the same answer as a call at noon. Say whether the water is still coming in and what it looks like, and dispatch will tell you what to do while a crew is arranged.

Before we arrive

Look at the pump

If a sump pump has stopped, check the power and discharge line only from dry footing.

Stay away from live power

If water is near outlets, the furnace or the panel, call the utility or 911 about the risk first.

Raise valuables

Lift papers, photos and electronics onto shelves, then photograph the water line.

Treat river water as dirty

Groundwater and river water are not clean. Keep children and pets out and wear boots and gloves if you must enter.

Groundwater on the river's west bank

In the April 2026 radio report on high river water, the mayor explained the basement risk this way: the water underground fills the water table, and people close to the river can see water arrive through the basement floor. West bank homes near the water should read that as a reason to test the sump pump before the spring thaw and not after it.

Seepage like this is not a plumbing leak, and it is commonly excluded from a standard homeowners policy. A pump that keeps up is the first defense and a documented loss is the second. We have not verified a FEMA flood zone for Centralia as a whole, so the FEMA Flood Map Service Center is the place to check a single address.

The cold months on this side of the river

Water damage here is not only a spring story. The UW-Madison State Climatology Office counts an average of 33 nights a year at or below zero degrees Fahrenheit in Wood County. A supply line in an exterior wall can freeze on such a night and split when it thaws, so the damage often shows up the next day.

If a faucet runs slow in a deep freeze, open the cabinet doors under the sink and call a plumber. If a pipe has already split, the frozen pipe page explains what happens at the thaw. Older or newer, any home with lines in an unheated space can be affected.

Centralia questions

Is emergency water removal available in Centralia at night or on weekends?

Yes. The dispatch line runs 24 hours a day, every day, and crews are sent from Wisconsin Rapids. Call when water is inside and say whether it is rising.

Is Centralia a separate town?

No. It was a separate city until 1900 and is part of Wisconsin Rapids today. Dispatch works from the street address, so give it when you call.

Is the west bank in a flood zone?

This page does not state a flood zone for the west bank. Enter your address in the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for that answer. A map result does not rule out seepage or a plumbing leak.

Is groundwater seepage covered by homeowners insurance?

Often not. Seepage and flood are commonly excluded from a standard policy, so check yours and ask your agent before you assume.

Can river water in a basement be dried in place?

Usually the porous materials are removed because the water is treated as contaminated. Hard surfaces and framing can be cleaned and dried.
