Water Damage Restoration on the East Side of Wisconsin Rapids, WI

The east side of Wisconsin Rapids began as the separate city of Grand Rapids. The Wisconsin Historical Society describes the present city as a consolidation of Grand Rapids on the east side of the river and Centralia on the west, completed in the spring of 1900. The name stayed Grand Rapids until 1920. No source we found gives an official east side boundary, so this page works from landmarks and streets.

Along the east bank the city lists Henry Demitz Park, Veterans Memorial Park and Woods Grove Park, which is also the East River Bank Conservancy. Legion Park at 931 1st Street North is the former Eastside Swimming Pool site, and Robinson Park at 1150 17th Street North is on the northeast side. Howe Elementary at 221 8th Street North is another reference point for the residential streets.

When water is spreading on the east side

A wet floor is an emergency when water is still rising, is reaching the furnace or panel, or has come up through a floor drain. Dispatch is open 24 hours a day, every day, so a basement that starts filling at midnight is a reason to call right then. Say what you see and whether the water looks clean, dirty or like sewage, because that decides what the crew brings.

Before we arrive

Check the sump pump

If you have one and it has stopped, look at the power and the discharge line from a dry spot.

Keep off the electrical

Do not step into water near the panel, the furnace or outlets. Call the utility or 911 about the risk.

Lift and photograph

Raise boxes, electronics and papers, then photograph the water line before anything is moved.

Leave sewage alone

A household vacuum is not made for standing water and it is not safe for sewage. Keep children and pets away.

How storm water reaches east side basements

The City of Wisconsin Rapids says basements can flood during storms because some storm sewer leads and basements are directly connected without backflow prevention, and that many homes lack foundation drainage that protects against those storms. Its own list of fixes starts with a sump pit and also includes a backwater valve in a floor drain. That guidance is citywide, so it applies here without being a record of east side losses.

The city's 2010 plan places the east side in the Fourmile and Fivemile Creek watersheds and says flooding has occurred during exceptionally heavy rainfall. We found no dated east side flood event, so this page does not call any street flood prone. We have also not verified a FEMA flood zone for the east side as a whole. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center answers that for one address at a time.

Streets with homes close together

The city's Safe Routes to School plan notes sidewalks and crossings in the high housing density areas near Howe Elementary. Where homes, duplexes or apartments sit close together, a failure in one unit can reach another, so tell your landlord or neighbor early if water crosses a wall or a ceiling.

Documented data on the age of east side homes was not available, so this page makes no claim about it. Plumbing that is decades old deserves attention wherever it is, and a new stain on a ceiling or a damp patch on a basement wall is worth a call. Find the likely path on the burst pipe page and the water heater leak page.

Watershed and rainfall statements are from the city's 2010 plan, the basement backup statement is from the city stormwater page, and the housing density note is from the Safe Routes to School plan.

How the work runs

  1. 1

    Call and describe

    Dispatch asks about the water, the electrical risk and whether a drain is involved.

  2. 2

    Safety check and extraction

    The crew checks the area, then removes standing water with extraction equipment.

  3. 3

    Remove and dry

    Wet drywall, pad and insulation are removed where they cannot be dried, and air movers and dehumidifiers are set.

    Verify and rebuild

    Moisture readings are tracked until dry, then repairs begin.

Insurance rules differ by cause. Sudden pipe or appliance failures are often covered, while seepage and flood are commonly excluded and sewer backup usually needs an added endorsement. Check your own policy and ask your insurer. The crew keeps photos, readings and a written scope so the loss can be reviewed.

What affects the price on the east side

East side questions

Is emergency water removal available on the east side at night?

Yes. The dispatch line runs 24 hours a day, every day, and crews are sent from Wisconsin Rapids. Call when water is inside and say whether it is rising.

Where does the east side end?

Dispatch works from the address and not from a map boundary. Call and give the street address, and dispatch will confirm it is within reach.

Is a sewer backup covered by my homeowners policy?

Often not on its own. Sewer backup usually needs an endorsement, so ask your agent before you assume. Photograph the damage before anything is moved.

Should I install a backwater valve or a sump pit?

A plumber can advise on that. The city lists a sump pit as its recommended fix and a backwater valve in a floor drain as another option.

Is the east side in a flood zone?

This page does not state a flood zone for the east side. Enter your address in the FEMA Flood Map Service Center to see how it is mapped, and remember that a map result does not rule out a leak or a sewer backup.
