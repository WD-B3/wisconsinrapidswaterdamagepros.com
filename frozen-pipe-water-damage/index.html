Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage in Wisconsin Rapids, WI

A frozen pipe often does its damage when it thaws, not when it freezes. Here is what to do if a line has split or may split.

Frozen pipe water damage in Wisconsin Rapids comes from supply lines that freeze, expand and crack. The crack often stays plugged by ice until the pipe thaws, so the flood appears when the weather warms or the heat comes on. Our winters are long enough that this is a regular cause of water losses here.

This page is about the freeze-specific problem. The cleanup after any burst line is on burst pipe water damage, and the equipment that dries the house is on structural drying. A basement hit by a thaw is on basement water damage cleanup.

A thawing pipe is an emergency

As soon as the ice melts, water pours through the split. That makes the thaw the dangerous moment, and it often happens when nobody is watching the pipe. Dispatch is open around the clock, including during cold snaps when many calls come at once.

If you suspect a frozen or split pipe

  1. Close the main water valve before you try to thaw anything.
  2. Never use an open flame, a torch or a propane heater on a pipe. Use warm air from a hair dryer, and keep it away from standing water.
  3. Open the nearby faucet to let melting water drain, and check the pipe for cracks or a bulge.
  4. If water is already leaking, call (715) 227-0967 at once.

Signs a pipe has frozen

No water or a trickle

A tap that suddenly runs weak or dry in cold weather.

Frost on the pipe

A visible layer of frost on an exposed line.

A bulge or crack

Swelling or a split in the pipe wall.

A smell from the drain

Odor from a drain can point to a frozen waste line.

How cold it gets here

The UW-Madison State Climatology Office reports an average of 33 nights per year at or below 0 degrees Fahrenheit in Wood County for 2006 to 2025. The first fall freeze comes on average around October 7, and the last spring freeze around May 4, for 1995 to 2024. That is roughly seven months in which a pipe in an exterior wall, a crawl space, a garage or an unheated porch can freeze.

Source for these figures

Both figures come from the State Climatology Office Wood County climate profile.

How cleanup works after a freeze

  1. 1

    Stop and drain

    The main valve is closed and the lines are drained.

  2. 2

    Extract and open

    Standing water is removed and wet cavities are opened.

  3. 3

    Dry and monitor

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings are normal.

  4. 4

    Repair

    A plumber fixes the pipe, and the structure is rebuilt once dry.

Winter drying has its own challenge. Cold weather limits what the crew can do with open doors and windows, and heat is needed to dry the materials. The crew keeps the building closed and the heat steady, and checks that the pipes that did not break do not freeze while the walls are open.

Frozen pipes and insurance

Burst pipes from freezing are commonly covered as sudden damage, though policies can have conditions about keeping the home heated. Check your policy and ask your agent. Photograph the pipe, keep the damaged section and do not make permanent repairs until your insurer approves.

Frozen pipe questions

Do you handle frozen pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Wisconsin Rapids?

Yes. Dispatch answers 24 hours a day, every day. Cold snaps are some of the busiest times, so call as soon as you see a problem.

Should I thaw a frozen pipe myself?

Only if there is no crack. Close the main valve first, use gentle warm air and never use a flame.

Why did the flood happen after the thaw?

Ice can plug a split pipe. When it melts, water flows through the crack at full pressure.

How can I prevent freezing?

Insulate exposed lines, seal drafts, keep the heat on and open cabinet doors on very cold nights.

Who repairs the pipe?

A plumber repairs the pipe. Restoration handles the water and the damage it caused.
