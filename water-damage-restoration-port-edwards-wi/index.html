When water is spreading in Port Edwards

A wet floor in Port Edwards is an emergency when water is still running, is reaching walls or outlets, or has come up through a drain. Before we arrive

Stop the source

Stay clear of electricity

If water is near outlets or the panel, keep out and call the utility or 911 about the risk first.

What the problem usually looks like here

The most common calls in a village of this size are plumbing failures and basements. A supply line fails in an older wall, a water heater gives out, or a sump pump stops during a spring thaw. Each of those is a different job. A split line is covered on the burst pipe page, a failed tank on the water heater leak page, and a wet lower level on basement water damage cleanup.

An older village with a river at its edge

Port Edwards began around a sawmill built about 1829, was named in 1869 for mill owner John Edwards, and became a village in 1902. Its Edwards and Clinton Company converted to papermaking by 1896.

The village still has a preserved cluster of homes built between the 1890s and the 1940s, and houses of that age are where aging supply lines, cast iron drains and old wiring turn a small leak into a wide one. If your home is in that range, treat any new stain as worth a call.

The Alexander House Center for Art and History at 1131 Wisconsin River Drive sits on the river bank, founded in 1990 to hold the historical collection of the former Nekoosa-Edwards Paper Company. It is a reminder that this village has lived beside the river for generations.

Homes near the bank should know their flood zone, and the FEMA Flood Map Service Center will tell you whether a specific address is mapped as a flood hazard. The wider flood story for the area is on the flood damage cleanup page.
