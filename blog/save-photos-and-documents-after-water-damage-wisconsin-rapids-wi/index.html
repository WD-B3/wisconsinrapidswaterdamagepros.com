How to Save Photos and Documents After Water Damage in Wisconsin Rapids

Wet photographs and paper deteriorate quickly, but many can be saved. The key is to keep them from drying in a clump and to start within a day or two.

By the Wisconsin Rapids Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

Photographs and paper documents are among the things people most want to save after a water loss. They are also among the most fragile. Wet paper tears, ink runs, and photographs stick to each other and to glass. The first day or two decides most outcomes, and the methods here are the ones used by archivists and conservators.

Treat this as general guidance. For irreplaceable items or large collections, a professional document recovery service has freeze-drying and other tools that homeowners do not.

First, make the area safe

Before you handle anything, stop the source and make sure the space is safe. If the water was sewage or flood water, wear gloves and a mask, and treat the items as contaminated. Contaminated items are usually discarded unless they are irreplaceable, in which case a professional should handle them. The water extraction page and the flood damage page cover the safety steps.

The 48 hour window

Mold can begin on wet paper within a couple of days, so work quickly. If you cannot dry items right away, freezing stops the clock. Place wet items in a freezer, separated by wax paper if possible, and deal with them later. A home freezer works for small amounts.

Step by step for photographs

  1. 1

    Separate the prints

    Do it under clean, cool water if they are stuck. Be gentle.

  2. 2

    Rinse off dirt

    Use clean water and do not rub the image.

  3. 3

    Dry flat

    Lay them face up on clean paper, out of direct sun.

  4. 4

    Store when dry

    Keep them in a cool, dry place with archival sleeves if possible.

Step by step for paper

Remove paper from wet boxes and folders. Do not try to separate pages that are stuck together, since that can tear them. Freeze them as a block if you cannot dry them quickly. For lightly damp documents, place paper towels between pages and change them as they dampen. Keep the room cool and the air moving, with a dehumidifier running.

Protecting what you saved

Papers and photographs dry best in a cool, dry room. That means the building itself must be dry. If the room is still damp, items will dampen again. The structural drying page explains how the room gets to a safe humidity.

Replace what you can

For vital records, you can request replacements. Birth and marriage certificates come from the state vital records office, and your bank and insurer can provide copies of statements and policies. Before you throw away wet papers, photograph them and list what they were. For insurance, keep the inventory and photographs of the damaged items.

What this means in Wisconsin Rapids

Papers stored in basements and garages are the usual casualties. The UW-Madison State Climatology Office lists a Wood County record of 5.17 inches of rain in one day, and high groundwater in spring can push water into lower levels. Move important papers above floor level in sealed plastic bins, and keep digital copies somewhere else.

If your house in Vesper or Plover has a basement, check where the papers are stored before the thaw. A fireproof box is not always waterproof, so look for one that is rated for both.

Going forward: make digital copies

The best protection for photos and papers is a copy. Scan important documents and photographs, and keep the files in two places, such as a drive at home and an online account. A phone photo of each page is better than nothing, and it takes minutes.

Store the originals in sealed plastic bins on a shelf, not on a basement floor. Silica gel packs help in damp rooms, and labeling bins by category makes them quick to grab if you have to leave in a hurry. Review the setup once a year, before the spring thaw.

Label what you save as you go. Write the date and a short note on a piece of paper placed with each batch, such as the room and the kind of water. If you later need to explain to an insurer or a recovery service what was wet and for how long, those notes answer the question without guesswork.

Photo and document questions

Where should I store papers in a Wisconsin Rapids home?

Above floor level in sealed plastic bins, away from the basement floor and exterior walls, with digital copies kept somewhere else.

Can I air dry photos on a towel?

Use clean paper instead. Towels leave fibers on the surface.

Is freezing really safe for photos?

It stops mold and buys time, especially when items are separated with wax paper.

Should I use a hair dryer?

No. Heat can warp paper and curl photographs.

What if the water was sewage?

Handle only with protection, and consider professional recovery for irreplaceable items.

Who recovers documents professionally?

Document recovery services use freeze-drying. Ask your insurer which ones they work with.
