Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 Explained for Wisconsin Rapids

Not all water is the same. The category tells you whether materials can be dried or must go, and it can change with time.

By the Wisconsin Rapids Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

Call Dispatch
(715) 227-0967
☎ Call Now

Restoration crews sort water into three categories, and the category decides what can be saved. The categories come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, the published guide for professional water damage work. Understanding them helps you read an estimate, talk to your insurer and decide what to do before a crew arrives.

The categories describe contamination, not color. Clear water can be category 2 or 3 if it came from a contaminated source. And water that looks cloudy can be category 1 if it came from a clean line and was caught quickly.

The three categories

CategorySource examplesGeneral handling
Category 1Supply lines, tanks, rainwater from a clean sourceExtract and dry in place for most materials
Category 2Washing machine and dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow without fecesExtract, remove porous materials as needed, dry
Category 3Sewage, river flood water, standing water with bacteriaRemove porous materials, clean and sanitize, dry

Category 1: clean water

This water comes from a sanitary source, like a burst supply line or a failed water heater. It does not pose a substantial health risk at first. Materials can usually be extracted and dried in place when the water is caught quickly. Examples are on the burst pipe page and the water heater page.

Category 2: some contamination

Water from a washing machine, a dishwasher or an overflowing sink can carry soap, food residue and microbes. It can make people sick if swallowed. Carpet pad and similar porous materials are often removed, while carpet and hard surfaces may be cleaned and dried. The decision depends on how long the water sat and what it touched.

Category 3: grossly contaminated

Sewage, river flood water and water from rising ground are category 3. They carry bacteria, viruses and other harmful material. Porous materials that touched them are usually discarded, and hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and sanitized. This is covered on the sewage backup page and the flood damage page.

How water moves between categories

Water can slide down the scale. A category 1 leak that sits for a day or two in a damp room can pick up bacteria from carpet, soil or building materials, and it may be treated as category 2. A category 2 loss that lingers can become category 3. That is a main reason speed saves money and materials.

  1. 1

    Time

    The longer water sits, the more it picks up.

  2. 2

    Temperature

    Warm rooms accelerate bacterial growth.

  3. 3

    Contact with materials

    Water passing through dirty carpet or soil degrades.

  4. 4

    Source

    The original source sets the starting category.

What each category means for your belongings

Hard items like glass, metal and sealed wood can usually be cleaned. Porous items such as books, cardboard, upholstered furniture and mattresses are the issue. After category 1, many can be dried. After category 2, some can. After category 3, nearly all are discarded. Take photographs before throwing anything away, and keep the records for your insurer.

How this plays out around Wisconsin Rapids

River and groundwater are category 3 situations in practice. In April 2026 the Wisconsin River went over its banks in the city, and the mayor warned that groundwater could push water into basements near the river. That kind of water should be treated as contaminated. The Wisconsin DNR notes that Wisconsin has seen a significant flood at least once in every decade since 1880, so it is a recurring concern and not a one-time event.

Out in Nekoosa and Port Edwards, homes near the bank have the same exposure. The sump pump and floor drain are the entry points that matter, and the basement page explains the response.

How a crew confirms the category on site

The crew asks where the water came from, how long it has been there and what it crossed on the way. It looks at the smell, the color and the debris, and checks whether the water touched soil, sewage or flood sources. The answer goes into the written scope, and it can change as the job proceeds.

If you are unsure, say so when you call. Treating water as contaminated costs a little more time, while treating contaminated water as clean can spread illness and leave mold behind. Crews lean toward caution because the risks are not equal.

Keep one practical rule in mind. When you cannot tell, treat the water as the higher category until the crew says otherwise. Keep children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system out of the area, wear gloves if you must touch anything, and wash your hands afterward.

Water category questions

Is river water in Wisconsin Rapids category 3?

In practice, yes. River and groundwater flooding is treated as contaminated, so porous materials that soaked it up are usually removed.

Is rainwater category 1?

Rain that comes through a roof can pick up debris and insulation material, so crews often treat it with care.

Who decides the category?

The restoration crew assesses it on site, and the estimate should state it.

Can category 3 carpet be cleaned?

Usually it is removed. Bacteria in the fibers and pad cannot be fully removed.

Does the color of the water matter?

Not alone. The source and the time decide the category.

What should I do if I am unsure?

Treat the water as contaminated, keep people out and call dispatch.
