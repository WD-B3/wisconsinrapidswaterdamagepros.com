What to Keep, Clean or Throw Away After Water Damage in Wisconsin Rapids

After a water loss you face a pile of wet things. The water type and the material decide what you can save, and what you should photograph first.

By the Wisconsin Rapids Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

☎ Call Now

After a water loss, the question is what to keep. The answer depends on the water type, how long things were wet and what they are made of. Hard, non-porous items can usually be cleaned. Soft, porous items are often the problem. This guide gives a practical sort and a way to protect your insurance claim.

Start by finding out the water category, since it changes the answer for nearly every item. The categories are explained on the sewage backup page for the most serious case.

Photograph first, then sort

Before you throw anything away, take photographs. The Wisconsin Office of the Commissioner of Insurance advises people to photograph everything and to keep damaged items until the adjuster agrees, when that is practical. If an item must go for health reasons, such as after sewage, photograph it, note the brand and age, and keep a tag or label.

Hard surfaces and structure

Framing, concrete and tile can usually be cleaned and dried, even after contaminated water. Drywall, insulation and carpet pad usually cannot be cleaned and are removed. Crews document what was removed and why, which supports your claim. The details by material are on the wall and drywall page and the hardwood page.

Food and kitchen items

  1. 1

    Discard food touched by flood water

    That includes cans with dents and anything in a container with a screw cap.

  2. 2

    Check the refrigerator

    If power was out or water reached the unit, follow food safety guidance.

  3. 3

    Sanitize hard dishes

    Wash in hot water and sanitize with a bleach solution as directed by health agencies.

  4. 4

    Discard porous items

    Wooden cutting boards and spoons can hold contamination.

Electronics and appliances

Do not power on anything that got wet, including appliances, tools and electronics. Unplug it, dry the outside, and have it checked. Water can damage components that look fine, and a shock risk remains. Appliances that touched flood water, like a furnace or water heater controls, should be inspected before use.

Using local knowledge

In Wisconsin Rapids, many losses involve a basement. Stored items in boxes on a basement floor are the most commonly lost. In April 2026 the Wisconsin River went over its banks in places, and the mayor warned that groundwater could reach basements near the river. If your house is close to the water, store keepsakes and documents above the floor in plastic bins.

A flooded basement in Nekoosa or Port Edwards can mean a pile of sorted items by the curb. A restoration crew can advise what to keep and what to discard. The basement page describes the work on a below-grade loss.

Cleaning methods for what you keep

Wash hard items with hot water and detergent, rinse and then sanitize with a diluted bleach solution as public health guidance describes. Let them air dry fully. Wash fabrics on the hottest setting the label allows, and dry them completely. Clothes that smell musty after drying should be washed again or replaced.

Wear gloves and boots when you sort items touched by flood or sewage water. Keep children and pets away from the pile, and wash your hands often. If anyone gets a cut while cleaning, treat it promptly and watch for signs of infection.

Disposal and hauling

Wet, heavy items are hard to move and can be messy. Bag small items and keep a list as you go. Large items like mattresses, carpet and drywall may need special pickup or a trip to a disposal site, and rules vary, so check with your municipality. Do not put wet material on the curb until you have photographed it and your adjuster has what is needed.

A restoration crew often removes and hauls the structural material it takes out, such as wet drywall and carpet pad, as part of the job. Ask what is included in the scope so you know what you are responsible for.

Keep or toss questions

Where can I check my flood risk in Wisconsin Rapids?

The FEMA Flood Map Service Center lets you enter an address and see whether it is mapped as a flood hazard. Homes near the Wisconsin River should check before the spring thaw.

Can I wash flooded clothes?

Clean water losses, yes. After sewage, hot washing may work, but heavily soaked items are often discarded.

Should I throw out all food?

Anything touched by flood or sewage water, yes. Follow public health guidance for the rest.

What about family photos?

Dry them carefully, and consider professional document recovery for important ones.

How do I handle an item I am not sure about?

Photograph it, set it aside and ask your adjuster or the crew.

Do I need to keep everything for my adjuster?

Keep what is practical, and rely on photographs and notes for what is not.
