The line is open 24 hours a day, every day of the year.

What we do

We cover Wisconsin Rapids, Port Edwards, Rudolph, Nekoosa, Vesper and Plover. The work includes water extraction, drying, removal of damaged materials and rebuilding. Our service pages describe 19 kinds of water loss, from burst pipes to flooded basements, in plain language.

What we will not do

We do not promise exact arrival times, because weather and other emergencies change them. We do not quote a price from a phone description, since the same leak can be a small job or a large one. We do not tell you what your insurance will cover, because that depends on your policy. We tell you to read it and call your agent.

What happens when you call

The call starts with safety. The person who answers asks whether the water is still running, whether it is near electrical equipment, and whether it looks clean, dirty or like sewage. Those three answers decide what to tell you to do in the next few minutes and what kind of crew and equipment to arrange.

Next come the details a crew needs: the address, the type of building, the rooms affected and the source if you know it. If you are unsure, say so. A crew would rather learn that on the phone than discover it on arrival.

Finally, dispatch tells you what to expect. Timing depends on where crews are, the weather and how many emergencies are open, so you hear a plain answer and not a promise that cannot be kept. You also hear how the scope and price will be explained once the crew sees the damage.

The crews behind the line

The crews dispatched through this line are licensed and insured restoration contractors. They work from moisture readings and written scopes, and they follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. You deal with the contractor directly on the scope of work, and you decide whether to go ahead.

Why we write the way we do

The guides on this site cite their sources and say plainly when something depends on your policy, your building or the water itself. Local figures come from public sources such as the UW-Madison State Climatology Office, the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources, the Wisconsin Office of the Commissioner of Insurance and the cities and villages themselves. Where we could not find a reliable source, we left the claim out.

We also avoid promises that no one can keep. We do not say how fast a crew will arrive before we know where it is, what a job will cost before the water has been seen, or what your insurer will pay before your policy has been read.

What the area asks of a restoration crew

Central Wisconsin brings a predictable set of water problems. Long winters mean frozen lines, with the State Climatology Office counting 33 nights a year at or below 0 degrees Fahrenheit in Wood County. Spring brings melt and high groundwater near the Wisconsin River. Summer storms can overwhelm drains, and the county record of 5.17 inches of rain in a day shows how hard a single storm can hit.

Housing ranges from century-old homes with plaster walls to new subdivisions with finished basements, and from village streets to farms on private wells. A crew has to be ready for all of it, and that is the reason the service pages separate the kinds of loss instead of treating every flood the same.
