Flood damage cleanup in Wisconsin Rapids deals with water that came from outside the plumbing: the Wisconsin River over its banks, groundwater pushing up through a floor, or heavy rain running toward a foundation. That water is usually contaminated, which is why it is handled differently from a burst pipe. If it is happening now, read the safety steps below and then call the 24/7 line.

This page covers rising and surface water. Water from a split supply line belongs on the burst pipe page, a failed sump pump or seepage in the lower level belongs on basement water damage, and wind-driven rain through the roof or siding belongs on storm water damage.

When flood water is an emergency

Flood water is an emergency the moment it is inside the living space or near anything electrical. It can carry sewage, fuel, soil and debris, so treat it as contaminated until a professional says otherwise. Dispatch answers 24/7, and the person on the line will talk you through safe steps while a crew is arranged.

Before you call from a flooded home

  1. Leave the area if water is still rising. Do not wade through it to save belongings.
  2. Stay away from the panel, outlets and cords if water is near them. Call the utility or 911 about an electrical or gas risk before you call for cleanup.
  3. Keep children and pets out of the water and do not run the furnace or appliances that are standing in it.
  4. If it is safe, photograph the waterline on the walls, then call (715) 227-0967.

Signs the water is flood water and not a leak

It came from outside

Water entered through a door, window well, floor drain or foundation wall instead of a pipe.

It looks or smells dirty

Brown or gray water, a sour smell, or a film on surfaces points to contamination.

It rose over hours

A steady rise after rain or high river levels is surface or groundwater.

A waterline marks the walls

A flat line of staining across several rooms shows how deep the water stood.

How flood water differs from a leak

Restoration crews sort water into three categories using the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, the published industry guide for water damage work. Category 1 comes from a clean source, category 2 carries some contamination, and category 3 is grossly contaminated. River, groundwater and surface flooding are usually treated as category 2 or 3, which changes what can be dried in place and what has to be removed.

Water sourceTypical categoryWhat usually follows
Supply line or tank leakCategory 1Extraction and drying in place for most materials
Washer or dishwasher overflowCategory 1 or 2Extraction, then drying, with some porous materials removed
River, groundwater or surface floodingCategory 2 or 3Porous materials removed, structure cleaned and dried
Sewage backupCategory 3All affected porous materials removed, see sewage backup cleanup

What flooding looks like around Wisconsin Rapids

The river is the obvious factor. In April 2026 the Wisconsin River ran over its banks in several places and parks near the water flooded, and the mayor noted that groundwater could push water into basements close to the river. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address, because the map shows whether a property sits in a mapped flood zone and your insurer will ask the same question. Two areas close to the water have their own pages, downtown Wisconsin Rapids and Centralia on the west bank.

Rain alone can do it too. The State Climatology Office lists a Wood County record of 5.17 inches of rain in a single day, at Babcock on September 22, 2016, which shows how fast a storm can overwhelm ground and drains.

Wisconsin has seen a significant flood at least once in every decade since 1880, according to the state Department of Natural Resources, so a quiet year is not a reason to skip a sump pump test. Homes near the river in Nekoosa and Port Edwards face the same exposure.

How flood cleanup runs

  1. 1

    Safety and scope

    The crew checks power, gas and contamination first, then maps where the water reached.

  2. 2

    Extraction

    Pumps and extraction units remove standing water from floors and low areas.

  3. 3

    Remove and clean

    Soaked carpet, pad, insulation and drywall below the waterline are removed and surfaces sanitized.

  4. 4

    Dry and rebuild

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings are normal, then repairs begin.

Moisture meters and thermal cameras show where water traveled behind walls. Drying is monitored on each visit and the equipment stays until the readings say the structure is dry. The structural drying page explains that monitoring step in detail.

What affects the price of flood cleanup

Flood jobs vary more than most losses, so the estimate is built from a few clear drivers. The crew explains each one on site and writes the scope down before work begins.

Depth and duration

Water that stood for days soaks deeper and affects more materials than water removed quickly.

Contamination level

Category 2 and 3 water adds removal, sanitizing and disposal steps.

Materials affected

Carpet, pad, drywall, insulation and trim below the waterline often cannot be saved.

Size and rebuild scope

More rooms and a larger rebuild change both the drying time and the repair work.

Flood damage and insurance

The Wisconsin Office of the Commissioner of Insurance says water that comes from the top down is generally covered by a standard homeowners policy, while water that comes from the bottom up, such as foundation seepage or overland flow, generally needs a flood policy.

New federal flood policies usually carry a 30 day waiting period, so coverage cannot be bought once water is already rising. Photograph the damage, keep damaged items until your adjuster agrees, and ask your insurer before permanent repairs begin.

Flood damage cleanup questions

Do you handle flood damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Wisconsin Rapids?

Yes. The dispatch line runs 24 hours a day, every day. Call as soon as water is inside and say whether it is rising, so the crew can plan around safety first.

Can flooded carpet and drywall be saved?

Usually not when the water was category 2 or 3. Porous materials that soaked up contaminated water are typically removed, while hard surfaces and framing can be cleaned and dried.

Is cleanup the same if the water came from rain and not the river?

The method is similar, but the source changes the safety checks and the insurance picture. Surface water is commonly treated as contaminated even when it looks clear.

Should I run my own fans and dehumidifier first?

Only after the standing water is out and any electrical risk is cleared. Household equipment cannot dry wall cavities or subfloors the way professional air movers and dehumidifiers can.

Do I need a permit to rebuild after a flood?

Permit rules vary, so ask the city building department. The City of Wisconsin Rapids states that permits are required for remodeling and alterations, and floodplain properties can have extra rules.
